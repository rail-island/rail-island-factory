## Published figures

- American one-man "bobtail" horsecar of the 1880s (John Stephenson Company, New York; J.G. Brill, Philadelphia; Wikipedia "Horsecar", "John Stephenson Company"): in service from about 1880; a body of 10-12 ft (3.0-3.7 m; the model 3.4 m), about 4.4 m over the front dash and the rear step, 1.9 m wide over the panels, about 2.6 m to the clerestory (approx.); 12-16 seated on two longitudinal benches, 16 in all (approx.); 8-10 km/h behind one horse or mule, the car about 1.5-1.8 t empty (approx.); track gauge 1435 mm (many lines used other gauges).
- The bobtail's features from the published descriptions: the front end closed, the driver standing on a small platform behind the dash, the passengers boarding by the rear door and step and paying into a fare box at the front bulkhead (no conductor).
- Read off the photographs (approx.): the deck (clerestory) roof with small lights and hooded ends reaching over the platforms, five to six windows a side with a top light over the sash rail, the yellow body with vermilion lined lower panels and the lettered board (Manchester 1877 postcard), the Stephenson single truck with 30 in (0.72-0.76 m) spoked wheels on about a 5 ft (1.5 m) wheelbase (Christchurch no. 1, Adelaide no. 18).
- Estimates (approx.): section lengths, window and roof heights, colours; the model rides on standard-gauge wheelsets (docs/models/loading-gauge.md).
